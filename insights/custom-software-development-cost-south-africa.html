How much does custom software development cost in South Africa?

Short answer: it depends on scope, but most business systems fall into a few recognisable price bands. At Auris Nexus, purpose-built business systems such as CRM, HR and leave management, booking or inventory platforms start from R30,000. Larger custom and enterprise platforms, including multi-tenant systems, integrations, mobile apps and legacy modernisation, start from R50,000. Professional business websites start from R12,500. Hosting, third-party services and ongoing support are budgeted separately.

This guide explains what moves a project from the bottom of a band to the top, the costs people forget, and how to get quotes you can actually compare.

What actually drives the price

  1. Number of user roles. A system used by one type of user is simpler than one used by employees, managers, HR and administrators, each seeing different screens and data.
  2. Workflow complexity. A single approval step is quick to build. Multi-level approvals, escalations, exceptions and audit trails take longer.
  3. Integrations. Connecting to accounting, payroll, CRM, payment gateways or government systems adds design, build and testing time, and depends on how good the other system's API is.
  4. Data migration. Moving years of spreadsheets into a clean database often takes more effort than expected, especially when the data is inconsistent.
  5. Multi-tenancy. A platform that several schools, branches or client organisations run independently needs extra architecture to keep their data and settings separate.
  6. Reporting. Standard lists are simple; custom dashboards and exports need design.
  7. Mobile apps. A native or cross-platform app is a separate product to design, build, test and publish.
  8. Security and compliance. POPIA, role-based access and audit logging are not optional for systems that hold personal information, and they need to be designed in from the start.

Practical examples

  • A leave management workflow for one organisation (request, approve, track balances, report) usually sits at the lower end of the business-system band. See the CIT Leave Management System case study.
  • A school management platform with admissions, learner records, attendance, fees and parent access, used by several schools, sits in the custom and enterprise band. See Funda360.
  • An operations workspace combining jobs, scheduling, fleet and incidents with role-based access also sits in the enterprise band, and is usually delivered in phases. See LOGIOS OS.

Costs people forget

  • Hosting and infrastructure: a monthly cost that depends on usage.
  • Third-party services: email sending, SMS, maps, AI models and payment gateways.
  • Support and maintenance: security updates, fixes and small changes. Care plans are listed on our pricing page.
  • Your team's time: discovery workshops, testing and training all need people from your side.

How to reduce cost without cutting corners

  • Phase the project. Build the part that removes the most manual work first, use it, learn from it, then build the next phase.
  • Configure before you build. If an existing product fits most of the need, configuring it can be cheaper. See build, buy or configure?
  • Start with discovery. A short technology consulting engagement defines the scope before anyone quotes a fixed price, so you don't pay for the wrong thing.

How to compare quotes

Ask every provider for:

  1. the scope in writing, including user roles, workflows and integrations;
  2. what is excluded;
  3. who owns the code and the data;
  4. hosting and support costs after launch;
  5. how changes in scope are priced.

A quote that is much lower than the others usually leaves something out. Ask what. Our guide on choosing a custom software development company covers the other questions worth asking.

Key takeaways

  • Business systems at Auris Nexus start from R30,000; custom and enterprise platforms from R50,000.
  • User roles, workflows, integrations and data migration move the price most.
  • Budget for hosting, third-party services and support, not only the build.
  • Phase the work and compare quotes on the same written scope.
The useful number is not the first invoice but the total cost of a system that solves the problem and keeps running.
