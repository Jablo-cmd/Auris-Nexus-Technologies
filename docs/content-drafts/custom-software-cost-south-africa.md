# How much does custom software development cost in South Africa?

**Short answer:** it depends on scope, but most business systems fall into a few recognisable bands. At Auris Nexus, purpose-built business systems such as CRM, HR and leave management, booking or inventory platforms start from **R30,000**. Larger custom and enterprise platforms, including multi-tenant systems, integrations, mobile apps and legacy modernisation, start from **R50,000**. Websites start from **R12,500**. You then budget separately for hosting, third-party services and ongoing support.

The rest of this guide explains what moves a project from the bottom of a band to the top, and how to get a quote you can compare.

## What actually drives the price

1. **Number of user roles.** A system used by one type of user is simpler than one used by employees, managers, HR and administrators, each seeing different screens and data.
2. **Workflow complexity.** A single approval step is quick to build. Multi-level approvals, escalations, exceptions and audit trails take longer.
3. **Integrations.** Connecting to accounting, payroll, CRM, payment gateways or government systems adds design, build and testing time, and depends on how good the other system's API is.
4. **Data migration.** Moving years of spreadsheets into a clean database often takes more effort than people expect, especially if the data is inconsistent.
5. **Multi-tenancy.** A platform that several schools, branches or client organisations run independently needs extra architecture for separation of data and settings.
6. **Reporting.** Standard lists are simple; custom dashboards and exports need design.
7. **Mobile apps.** A native or cross-platform app is a separate product to design, build, test and publish.
8. **Security and compliance.** POPIA, role-based access and audit logging are not optional for systems that hold personal information, and they need to be designed in.

## Practical examples

- **A leave management workflow** for one organisation (request, approve, track balances, report) is usually at the lower end of the business-system band.
- **A school management platform** with admissions, learner records, attendance, fees and parent access, used by several schools, sits in the custom and enterprise band.
- **An operations workspace** combining jobs, scheduling, fleet and incidents, with role-based access, also sits in the enterprise band and is often delivered in phases.

## Costs people forget

- **Hosting and infrastructure:** a monthly cost that depends on usage.
- **Third-party services:** email sending, SMS, maps, AI models, payment gateways.
- **Support and maintenance:** security updates, fixes, small changes. See the care plans on the [pricing page](/pricing.html).
- **Your team's time:** for discovery workshops, testing and training.

## How to reduce cost without cutting corners

- **Phase the project.** Build the part that removes the most manual work first. Use it, learn, then build the next phase.
- **Configure before you build.** If an existing product fits most of the need, configuring it can be cheaper. See [build, buy or configure?](/insights/build-buy-or-configure-business-software.html)
- **Start with discovery.** A short [technology consulting](/technology-consulting.html) engagement defines scope before anyone quotes a fixed price, which avoids paying for the wrong thing.

## How to compare quotes

Ask every provider for:
- the scope in writing, including user roles, workflows and integrations;
- what is excluded;
- who owns the code and data;
- hosting and support costs after launch;
- how changes in scope are priced.

A quote that is much lower than others usually leaves something out. Ask what.
