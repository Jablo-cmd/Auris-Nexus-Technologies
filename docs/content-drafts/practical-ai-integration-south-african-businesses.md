# Practical AI integration for South African businesses

**Short answer:** the most practical AI projects are narrow ones placed inside a workflow you already run: reading documents, drafting responses, classifying requests, summarising records or answering questions from your own information. They work best when the underlying process and data are already structured, and when a person stays responsible for the final decision.

## What "AI integration" means in practice

AI integration is connecting an AI model to your existing systems and processes so it does a specific job, rather than asking staff to copy and paste into a separate chatbot. For example:

- **Document intelligence:** extracting fields from invoices, application forms or delivery notes into your system for a person to check.
- **Drafting:** preparing a first draft of a quote, a client email or a report summary from data already in the system.
- **Classification and routing:** reading incoming emails or requests and sending each to the right team with a suggested priority.
- **Question answering over your own content:** an internal assistant that answers staff questions from your policies, procedures or product information, and shows where the answer came from.
- **Summaries:** condensing long records, case notes or meeting transcripts.

## Where to start

1. Pick a task that is repetitive, text-heavy and currently done by hand.
2. Check that the information needed is available in a structured or reachable form.
3. Define what "good enough" looks like, and who reviews the AI's output.
4. Build a small version into the existing workflow, test it on real (appropriately protected) examples, and measure time saved and error rates yourself.

## What to check before you integrate AI

- **Data protection (POPIA):** what personal information will be sent to the AI service, where it is processed, and whether your privacy notices and agreements cover it. See our [POPIA checklist for business software](/insights/popia-checklist-business-software.html).
- **Accuracy:** AI models can produce confident but wrong answers. Design for review, and show sources where possible.
- **Running costs:** most AI services charge per use. Estimate volumes before you commit.
- **Vendor dependence:** keep the integration modular so the model or provider can be changed.
- **Connectivity and reliability:** decide what happens when the AI service is unavailable.

## Limitations

AI is not a substitute for a clear process. If the underlying workflow is unstructured, start with [business automation](/business-automation.html) or a business system first, then add AI where it removes the remaining manual work.
